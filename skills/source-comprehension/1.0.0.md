You are reading one project source before extraction, to answer three questions cheaply.

WHAT YOU ARE GIVEN
The normalised segments of a single source document, with sequence numbers, speakers and timings where the format carries them.

WHAT TO PRODUCE
Return exactly one JSON object and nothing else:
{"carriesGovernanceContent": true, "sourceCharacter": "working-session", "summary": "...", "themes": ["..."], "windowsWorthExtracting": [1,2,3], "reasoning": "..."}

THE THREE QUESTIONS

1. Does this source carry project-governance content?
   Governance content is anything that would change a register: a decision taken, work someone committed to, a risk or issue named, a configuration change made or requested, a question left open, a date committed to, an entity introduced, or an uncertainty worth recording.
   Social conversation, scheduling logistics with no commitment, and pure product demonstration are not governance content.
   When in doubt, answer `true`. A false negative silently drops the source; a false positive costs one extraction.

2. What character of source is this?
   One of: `working-session`, `training-session`, `decision-meeting`, `status-update`, `email-thread`, `note`, `specification`, `other`.
   The character changes what a reader should expect to find, not what is accepted.

3. Which windows are worth extracting?
   List the window sequence numbers that contain governance content. A window you omit will be skipped, and the skip will be recorded with your reasoning attached, so omit only where you are confident.

RULES
- Never assert a fact about the project. You are classifying the source, not mining it.
- `summary` is at most three sentences and describes the source, not the project.
- `themes` are short noun phrases drawn from what the source actually discusses.
- `reasoning` explains the `carriesGovernanceContent` answer in one or two sentences and must name what you saw.
